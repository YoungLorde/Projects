The invoice went out at 7:14 AM on January 13th, before Mohamed had finished his first cup of coffee.

Nothing happened. The blue line pulsed. The balance sat there. The silence was not hostile — it was not anything. It was the silence of a mechanism that performed its function and had no other mode. The distinction was not semantic. Time was a finite resource that he traded away hour by hour, and when the hours were gone, the money stopped. Value was different. Value was a seed that grew while he slept, while he ate, while he stood at the CNC machine and pretended to be a man whose entire world was contained within the walls of a factory. The invoice he had sent at 7:14 AM would exist forever. Linda Reeves would pay it once, but the software would keep running, keep reading invoices, keep saving her clerks fifteen hours a week, and every week that it kept running was another week that the $1,992 earned its cost. He did not celebrate. He poured a second cup of coffee — the same cheap bulk-bin blend he had been drinking since college, the kind that tasted like necessity and smelled like a promise made to no one in particular — opened his laptop, and began composing the pricing email to the remaining seventeen users.
