# Wanderer Society & Culture

## What Makes a Wanderer
A Wanderer is a Traveller who has lost their Vehicle Core. The moment the core is lost, their **Soul Vehicle dematerializes permanently** — everything stored in the pocket dimension is gone unless separately stored in personal System Inventory. This can happen through:
- **Raid/theft**: Another Traveller forcibly extracts the core from the Wanderer's body (the core merges into the Traveller upon bonding — extraction is violent and traumatic)
- **Core destruction**: Catastrophic damage destroys the core entirely
- **Core rejection**: Extremely rare — the core rejects its soul bond (usually due to System anomaly)

The moment a Traveller loses their Vehicle Core, the System reclassifies them as a Wanderer. They can no longer summon their Soul Vehicle, access any vehicle upgrades, or use any core-dependent abilities.

## The Phantom Travelling Effect
Wanderers carry a psychological condition called **Phantom Travelling** — the ingrained habit of constant movement from their time as Travellers. Even without a vehicle, they feel the compulsion to keep moving. This manifests as:
- Restlessness when staying in one place too long
- Subconscious route-planning (always looking for exits, paths, next destinations)
- Sleep difficulty in permanent structures (many Wanderers sleep better outdoors)
- Phantom vehicle sensations (feeling the rumble of an engine, reaching for controls that aren't there)

Because of Phantom Travelling, most Wanderers drift from place to place on foot. They rarely settle permanently. This creates a nomadic underclass that flows between settlements.

## Wanderer Camps & Bases
Wanderers gather in temporary and semi-permanent communities:

### Drift Camps (Temporary)
- Last 1-3 days at a location
- 5-30 Wanderers traveling in loose groups
- Minimal infrastructure — tents, campfires, shared watches
- Form and dissolve constantly as members drift in and out
- Located near resource points (water, food, trade routes)

### Haven Bases (Semi-Permanent)
- Last months to years at a strategic location
- 50-500+ Wanderers
- Built infrastructure: walls, water collection, basic shelters, workshops
- Leadership: Usually the longest-resident or most experienced Wanderers
- Economy: Internal barter system, RP rarely used (most Wanderers are RP-poor)
- Located near dungeon entrances, trade routes, or abandoned infrastructure

### Anchor Towns (Permanent Wanderer Settlements)
- Rare but exist on major trade routes
- 1,000+ Wanderers
- Full infrastructure: markets, walls, guard rotations, craftsmen quarters
- Often develop symbiotic relationships with nearby Traveller cities
- Provide labor, scouting, and local knowledge in exchange for protection and supplies

## Wanderer Employment
Travellers and settlements regularly hire Wanderers for:

### Bodyguard Work
- Wanderers retain their personal stats and combat experience
- High-level Wanderers are formidable fighters even without vehicles
- Hired for personal protection, caravan escort, settlement defense
- Pay: RP, supplies, or rarely — vehicle core fragments (useless alone but psychologically valuable)

### Labor & Construction
- Building, mining, farming, scavenging
- Wanderers are often desperate and accept low pay
- Some settlements exploit this — near-slavery conditions in the worst cases
- Ethical settlements provide fair wages and basic living conditions

### Farming & Agriculture
- Post-System agriculture requires System-enhanced techniques
- Wanderers with farming knowledge are valuable
- Some Wanderer communities develop their own agricultural systems
- Crops can be System-enhanced through proper cultivation (literal cultivation)

### Scouting & Exploration
- Wanderers know terrain intimately from their travels
- Hired to scout dungeon entrances, map dangerous zones, find resources
- The most dangerous and best-paid Wanderer work
- High casualty rate — no vehicle means no mobile shelter, no quick escape

### Information Brokering
- Wanderers travel constantly and gather information
- A Wanderer network spans entire regions, passing news from camp to camp
- Some Wanderers specialize as information brokers
- The "Wanderer's Whisper" — a rumor network faster than any communication system

## Wanderer Social Status
- Wanderers are generally looked down upon by Travellers
- Losing one's Vehicle Core is seen as ultimate failure or humiliation
- Some cultures (notably the Varak'shi and Dravik) consider Wanderers dishonorable
- Other cultures (Nexari, Elari) view Wanderers with sympathy or respect
- Children born in Wanderer camps face the worst prospects — no core, no vehicle, minimal stats

## Children Born Without Vehicle Cores
- Children born within the Game System are NOT automatically bonded to a Vehicle Core
- They must be **provided** a core through one of three methods:
  1. **Hunted**: Kill a creature that drops a Vehicle Core (extremely rare, extremely dangerous)
  2. **Purchased**: Buy a Vehicle Core on the open market
     - **Even the LOWEST tier (Tier 0) Vehicle Core costs hundreds of millions of RP**
     - This is functionally impossible for most Wanderers
     - Only wealthy Travellers can afford to buy cores for their children
  3. **Inherited**: A dying Traveller can will their core to a child (risky — core may reject)

### The Core Gap
- Creates massive social inequality between core-holders and core-less
- Wealthy families buy cores for their children at birth
- Poor families and Wanderers may never afford a core
- Core-less children grow up as permanent Wanderers unless they find a core
- Black market core trading is a massive underground economy
- Some factions hoard cores as political/economic power

## Human Baseline Stats
A normal, healthy, athletic adult human with no health problems has stats of:
```
STR: 1  |  AGI: 1  |  END: 1  |  INT: 1  |  PER: 1  |  LCK: 1  |  SYS: 1
```
All stats start at 1 for baseline humans. This means:
- STR 1 = Can lift what a fit human can lift (~100kg deadlift)
- AGI 1 = Normal human reflexes and speed
- END 1 = Can run 5km without stopping, take a punch, recover normally
- INT 1 = Average adult intelligence and learning speed
- PER 1 = Normal human senses (20/20 vision, standard hearing)
- LCK 1 = No System luck modification (baseline probability)
- SYS 1 = Minimal System interaction capability

### Stat Scaling Reference
| Stat Value | Equivalent |
|------------|-----------|
| 1 | Healthy athletic human |
| 2-3 | Olympic athlete level |
| 5 | Beyond human capability |
| 10 | Superhuman |
| 15 | City-level threat |
| 20 | Regional-level threat |
| 30 | Continental-level threat |
| 50+ | Planetary-level threat |

People start with varying stats — some above 1, some at 1 — depending on:
- Species (non-human races have different baselines)
- Genetics (some humans are born with 2 in one stat)
- Environment (harsh environment births may grant +1 END)
- System assignment (the System occasionally grants bonus starting stats)

## EVA Suits & Space Travel
The universe is brutal. Space is unforgiving. Travellers need **EVA (Extra-Vehicular Activity) Suits** to survive outside their vehicles in space.

### Acquiring EVA Suits
There are only two ways:
1. **Research & Produce**: Requires significant INT investment, material resources, crafting infrastructure, and time. A Traveller or faction must:
   - Research space survival technology (INT check, research station needed)
   - Gather materials (System-enhanced fabrics, pressure seals, oxygen systems)
   - Manufacture the suit (crafting skill + workshop)
   - This takes weeks to months depending on tech level
2. **Purchase**: Buy from factions or traders who produce them
   - Extremely expensive — a basic EVA suit costs tens of millions of RP
   - Quality varies — cheap suits have limited oxygen, poor radiation shielding
   - Military-grade suits cost hundreds of millions

### EVA Suit Tiers
| Tier | Features | Duration | Cost (RP) |
|------|----------|----------|-----------|
| Basic | Pressure seal, 2hr oxygen, no radiation shield | 2 hours | 10M |
| Standard | Pressure + radiation, 8hr oxygen, basic thrusters | 8 hours | 50M |
| Advanced | Full protection, 24hr oxygen, maneuvering jets, HUD | 24 hours | 200M |
| Military | Combat-rated, 72hr life support, integrated weapons, shields | 72 hours | 1B+ |
| Explorer | Indefinite life support, self-repair, System-enhanced sensors | Indefinite | 5B+ |

### Why EVA Suits Matter to the Story
- Creates a barrier to space exploration for early-game characters
- Economic pressure — another thing that costs RP
- Life-or-death stakes when suits malfunction
- Social inequality — rich Travellers explore freely, poor ones are planet-bound
- Military advantage — factions with EVA suit production dominate space zones
